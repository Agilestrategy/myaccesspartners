Investor Residence Pathway

Everything the crew needs on how a wealthy Indian family obtains New Zealand residence by investment, keeps it, and (if they choose) becomes a New Zealand citizen. Every number on this page was checked against Immigration New Zealand, Invest New Zealand, the Department of Internal Affairs, Inland Revenue, LINZ, the Immigration Advisers Authority and the Reserve Bank of India on 17 September 2026. Sources are listed at the foot of the page.

There is no such thing as a New Zealand "golden passport"

New Zealand sells residence by investment. A passport requires citizenship, which comes only after 5 years of residence with real physical presence (1,350 days over 5 years, at least 240 days in every 12 months) and an English conversation test. India does not permit dual citizenship, so an Indian who takes a New Zealand passport surrenders the Indian one and moves to an OCI card. Never use the phrase "golden passport" in anything client facing.

1Categories, amounts and time in New Zealand

The visa is the Active Investor Plus (AIP) Resident Visa. It was reset on 1 April 2025 into two categories. The old Investor 1 (NZ$10M) and Investor 2 (NZ$3M) categories closed to new applicants on 28 July 2022, so any "NZ$3M" figure is obsolete. There is no weighting system, no points, no ballot, no English test, no age limit, no business experience requirement and no cap on the number of visas granted per year.

2What counts as an acceptable investment

Three tests apply to every dollar: it is not for personal use, it is invested in New Zealand in New Zealand dollars, and it is on the list for the chosen category. Value is measured when the investment is made, inclusive of management, brokerage and transaction fees (legal and advisory costs outside the investment do not count).

Growth category (NZ$5M)

  • Managed funds on the Invest NZ Acceptable Managed Funds List (section 4). A legally binding agreement to acquire units in a listed fund counts as "invested" from the date of commitment, even if the fund calls the capital later.
  • Direct investments into a privately held New Zealand business, confirmed in writing by Invest NZ before the money goes in. Since 25 October 2025 the business must show genuine investor interest: prior or committed capital from an approved fund, an unrelated wholesale investor, or the applicant. Approval letters last 12 months.
  • Philanthropy up to 20% (NZ$1M of NZ$5M) from 1 June 2026, into a charity with 5 years of compliant returns, Charities Services tier 1 to 3 and IRD donee status, or approved Department of Conservation projects.
  • Not acceptable in Growth: pure property (residential, commercial or industrial real estate), listed shares bought on market, bonds, cash, and Discretionary Investment Management Services (DIMS) for applications on or after 4 December 2025. Build to Rent becomes acceptable through approved managed funds only from December 2026.

Balanced category (NZ$10M)

  • All Growth assets, plus:
  • Listed equities of NZ resident entities on an FMA licensed exchange, FMA licensed crowdfunding, or NZ registered bank equity; ETFs and FMA licensed funds holding them (only the NZ portion of a mixed fund counts).
  • Bonds: NZ government and local authority; NZDX traded; NZ firms rated BBB minus or better; NZ registered bank bonds; qualifying finance company bonds. Perpetual preference shares and convertible notes are treated as bonds.
  • Property development: new residential developments of multiple dwellings for commercial return, new commercial or industrial developments, or existing commercial and industrial property being improved. House and land packages and off the plan purchases do not count. Neither the applicant nor relatives may live in it. Can be done through the applicant's own NZ company for applications on or after 9 June 2025 (no Invest NZ approval needed; INZ assesses at evidence stage).

Money waiting to be called ("on call" funds)

Where a fund has accepted a commitment but has not yet called the money, the uncalled amount must sit in NZ listed equities, bonds, NZ bank accounts or NZ term deposits, in NZ dollars. At most 25% of the committed amount (NZ$1.25M of NZ$5M) may be in a bank account or term deposit at any point. Withdrawals are only for capital calls, tax, fees, or moving between on call assets, and must complete within 30 days.

Sources: INZ "Acceptable investments for an Active Investor Plus" page; Immigration Instructions BN7.5, BN7.10, Appendix 15 (effective 4 Dec 2025); INZ news 25 May 2026 (philanthropy) and 9 Sep 2026 (Build to Rent).

3Step by step, decision to permanent residence

  1. Engage a licensed adviser or NZ lawyerWeek 0

    Only an Immigration Advisers Authority licensed adviser or a New Zealand lawyer with a current practising certificate may advise on which category fits, how to structure the nominated funds and how to answer the form. MAP introduces; it does not advise (section 11).

  2. Nominate funds and prove they are lawful and ownedWeeks 0 to 6

    Nominate at least NZ$5M (Growth) or NZ$10M (Balanced) of funds or assets. Show ownership and lawful acquisition with a paper trail. Gifts are acceptable only if unconditional and lawful where made; funds already in New Zealand cannot be gifted. Borrowed funds only from a bank or commercial lender, secured against the nominated assets, in the same jurisdiction as those assets. Police certificates for everyone 17 and over, medicals, passports.

  3. Lodge the online residence application and pay NZ$27,470Week 6

    Direct online application. There is no expression of interest stage and no ballot. Applications are given priority processing. Invest NZ's "Express your interest" form is an optional, non statutory way to get an Investor Migrant Manager assigned.

  4. Approval in principle80% within 4 months

    INZ issues an approval in principle letter. The clock now starts: the applicant has 6 months to transfer and invest. A work visa with multiple entry travel conditions for 12 months is available to come and arrange the investment; partner gets a visitor visa and children student visas for the same period.

  5. Choose and sign the investmentsInside the 6 months

    For a managed fund: sign a legally binding subscription agreement under New Zealand law that states the amount committed. For a direct investment: get Invest NZ's written confirmation first, then a legally binding, non revocable contract under New Zealand law. Category can be switched from Growth to Balanced (or extra funds nominated for currency loss) within this window.

  6. Transfer the money through the banking systemInside the 6 months (up to 12 with an extension)

    Funds must arrive by SWIFT or telegraphic transfer directly to New Zealand from the applicant's own account, a joint account, a solicitor's trust account, a pension scheme or an investment portfolio account. Documented, traceable, no physical cash. Since 12 August 2026, services that convert offshore and pay in as a domestic NZ transfer are expressly not accepted. One extension of up to 6 months is possible on evidence of reasonable attempts; 12 months from approval in principle is the absolute maximum, after which the application must be declined.

  7. Send the evidence of investmentWithin 3 months after the transfer window ends

    Fund name, amount in NZD, date, type, and a letter from a solicitor, chartered accountant, bank or broker, emailed to INZ's investor funds team. INZ checks the fund was on the Invest NZ list on the day of the transaction.

  8. Resident visa granted with section 49 conditionsInvestment period starts when all investments complete

    Conditions: keep the investments for 36 or 60 months; spend the minimum days in NZ as a resident; keep INZ informed of contact details. Family must arrive within 12 months. Travel conditions run 4 years (Growth) or 6 years (Balanced) from first arrival.

  9. 24 month checkpointWithin 3 months after month 24

    Evidence the investments are still held, plus the 24 month investment questionnaire. Capital returned early must be reinvested within 30 days (listed equities, bonds, property, philanthropy) or 90 days (funds and direct investments).

  10. End of period checkpointWithin 3 months after month 36 or 60

    Evidence for the final 12 months plus the final questionnaire. Failure to comply can make the holder liable for deportation.

  11. Section 49 conditions removedNo fee

    Cover letter, passports, a letter from an independent accountant or solicitor confirming the conditions were met, proof of NZ residential address. INZ confirms in writing.

  12. Permanent Resident VisaAfter 36 months Growth or 60 months Balanced · NZ$315 · 80% within 2 weeks

    Indefinite right to live in New Zealand with no travel conditions. This is the "keep it forever" point. Citizenship is a separate DIA process (section 6).

Sources: Immigration Instructions BN3, BN8.5, BN8.10 (12 Aug 2026), BN8.15, BN8.20, BN8.25, BN8.30, BN9.1, BN9.5, BN10.1, BN10.25; INZ AIP and Permanent Resident Visa pages.

4The approved funds and their status

This is the Invest New Zealand Acceptable Managed Funds List as published on 24 August 2026 (71 funds). Every fund on it is acceptable for the Growth category; Balanced applicants may use them too. Invest NZ publishes only the name, the date added, the fund type and the manager's own description. It does not publish a status column: a fund that is revoked or suspended is simply removed, so the only way to confirm status is to check the live list on the day (section 13). Close dates and "invite only" notes below come from the managers' own descriptions. Minimums are not published; most funds are wholesale offers, which in practice means NZ$750,000 or more per investor or an eligible investor certificate.

What the list means legally

Invest NZ's words: "At the time of assessing an application for an Active Investor Plus visa, Immigration New Zealand will assess the relevant investment transaction and determine if the Managed Investment Scheme was on the list at the time the transaction occurred." Invest NZ is a conduit only; it does not endorse any fund, and a fund may not describe itself as "government approved". Permitted wording is: "The Fund has been assessed by Invest NZ as meeting the eligibility criteria for the Growth category of the Active Investor Plus visa."

5How a fund qualifies, and where a Dubai fund or an Indian lawyer can sit

This is the section that matters for Tusshar Pawar. A fund manager does not need to be New Zealand owned, but the fund must be run and controlled from New Zealand and invested in New Zealand. There is no fee to apply and Invest NZ suggests 4 to 6 weeks from application to listing, once the 28 September 2026 freeze lifts.

The four legal criteria (Appendix 15, para 2)

  • 2.1 Managed by a fund manager and/or general partner that is appropriately registered and is a New Zealand Resident Entity: incorporated in NZ, head office in NZ, centre of management in NZ, and control by the company directors exercised in NZ.
  • 2.2 Holds the applicant's funds in New Zealand, in New Zealand dollars.
  • 2.3 Invests wholly or substantially in New Zealand Entities (at least 70% of initial net committed capital not allocated to other funds), and/or in other qualifying funds, none quoted on any exchange. Up to 30% may go to overseas managed funds.
  • 2.4 Meets the Objectives and Principles: contributes to the Government's economic strategy (technology, manufacturing, food and beverage, renewable energy, aged care, horticulture, forestry, agriculture, aquaculture, infrastructure including tourism, transport, digital, health, education and public infrastructure); invests into legitimate entities with proper verification; and does not prejudice New Zealand's reputation. Pure property is excluded from Growth.

What "New Zealand Entity" means for the investee

Majority of full time staff and contractors in NZ (or main operations in NZ), plus one of: voting control held by NZ tax residents, a majority NZ tax resident senior leadership team, or headquarters in NZ.

Invest NZ's application process for a fund

  • Apply through the Business Connect portal (RealMe login). No fee. No pre approval or informal guidance is given.
  • Gate questions: Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) registration (mandatory), NZBN, and confirmation of the 70% New Zealand Entities test.
  • Pre application pack: NZBNs for all related entities, FSP registration, general partner registered, signed trust deed or partnership agreement, conflicts of interest policy, ESG policy (mandatory), information memorandum, investment committee profiles, financial summary template for single asset funds.
  • Licensing: a wholesale only manager needs FSPR registration; a retail manager also needs an FMA managed investment scheme manager licence (fee NZ$3,565 plus NZ$178.25 per hour beyond 25 hours) and a licensed supervisor.
  • Character: every director, senior manager and major shareholder (over 25%) is declared; all litigation, insolvency and regulatory history must be disclosed with no time limit; nobody banned under the Companies Act, FMC Act, Takeovers Act or CCCFA.
  • Allow 4 to 6 weeks; requests unanswered for 60 days are treated as withdrawn; approved funds appear on the list within 3 business days; declined funds have no reconsideration but may reapply on material change.
  • Ongoing: quarterly re certification; mandatory disclosure of material changes within 10 business days; a fund that receives no AIP capital within 12 months may be reviewed; Invest NZ may suspend or revoke (effective immediately, earlier investors unaffected).
  • Deployment: closed funds must show a deployment plan; open ended private credit funds are watched for cash sitting undeployed. The 28 September 2026 changes are expected to tighten this further and add a stand down before a declined fund can reapply.

Where an offshore partner can sit

Nothing in Appendix 15, the FMC Act or the FSP Act prohibits foreign ownership of the manager. The constraints are: (1) the manager or general partner must be a NZ Resident Entity and a NZ entity must hold control and full discretion over deal sourcing, due diligence, investment decisions, portfolio management and compliance (analyst, administrative and back office work may sit offshore under a signed investment management agreement); (2) at least one director must live in New Zealand (or in Australia and be a director of an Australian company); (3) directors, senior managers and owners over 25% pass criminal history, banning and fit and proper checks; (4) the manager is an AML/CFT reporting entity; (5) the Overseas Investment Act is only engaged if the fund buys sensitive land or a business worth over NZ$100M. So a Dubai fund or a Mumbai law firm can be a shareholder, an introducer, a distribution partner and a back office, but not the decision maker. That is the structure to put in front of NZ counsel.

Wholesale investor status (why the NZ$750,000 figure keeps appearing)

Most approved funds are wholesale only, so they avoid the retail disclosure regime. An investor is "wholesale" under Schedule 1 of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 if, among other routes, the minimum amount payable on acceptance is at least NZ$750,000 (with a prescribed warning and written acknowledgement), or they hold an eligible investor certificate confirmed by a lawyer, chartered accountant or financial adviser, or they held NZ$1M or more of financial products in the last two years, or have net assets or turnover over NZ$5M. An AIP investor committing NZ$5M will qualify. The FMA is consulting on tightening the NZ$750,000 route.

Sources: Immigration Instructions Appendix 15 (opsmanual 78377, effective 4 Dec 2025) and BN7.10.20; Invest NZ Managed Funds Application Guidance Notes (13 Aug 2026) and Mandatory Disclosure Requirements (Feb 2026); FMA MIS manager licensing pages; Companies Office director residency rule; LINZ "who needs consent".

The arithmetic a Growth investor must hear

A Growth investor who spends only the 21 day minimum in New Zealand will hold a resident visa and then a permanent resident visa, but will never qualify for citizenship. Citizenship needs roughly 3.7 years of physical presence in a 5 year window. The realistic passport timeline for a family that actually moves is 5 years of residence plus 6 to 12 months of processing and ceremony: about 6 years from the resident visa.

India does not permit dual citizenship. Under section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955 an Indian who voluntarily acquires another citizenship ceases to be an Indian citizen automatically. The Indian passport must be surrendered for a Surrender Certificate (using or holding it afterwards is an offence under the Passports Act 1967). The family can then apply for Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) cards: lifelong multiple entry visa, no FRRO registration, parity with NRIs in economic, financial and educational matters, but no voting, no public office, and no purchase of agricultural or plantation land. New Zealand itself allows dual citizenship.

Sources: govt.nz citizenship requirements, fees (updated 28 Aug 2026), timeframes, ceremonies and citizenship test pages; govt.nz passport fees; INZ Permanent Resident Visa page; Citizenship Act 1955 (India) s9; MHA OCI brochure via Indian missions.

7Buying a home in New Zealand

Since 6 March 2026 (Overseas Investment (National Interest Test and Other Matters) Amendment Act, announced 1 September 2025), holders of an AIP, Investor 1 or Investor 2 visa, and those who moved to permanent residence from one, may buy or build one home worth more than NZ$5 million. Consent under the Overseas Investment Act is still required before purchase (the agreement must be conditional on consent; usually granted within 5 working days, statutory 15), the land must be residential or lifestyle and not otherwise sensitive, a build must complete within 3 years, and the property can be lived in, used as a holiday home or used to run a business. It does not count toward the visa investment. Each visa holding partner may rely on their own visa for one property. Fee NZ$2,040 for an existing house over NZ$5M, NZ$3,500 otherwise.

Sources: LINZ guidance for investor visa holders (26 Mar 2026); Beehive release 1 Sep 2025.

8New Zealand tax, in one paragraph each

  • Tax residency starts at more than 183 days in any 12 months (backdated to day one) or on having a permanent place of abode. A "non resident visitor" carve out allows up to 275 days in 18 months for people still taxed elsewhere and not working for NZ persons.
  • Transitional resident exemption: new residents who were not NZ tax resident in the previous 10 years pay no NZ tax on most foreign sourced income (interest, dividends, rents, foreign investment fund income) for about 4 years, once only. Foreign employment and personal services income is not exempt. Claiming Working for Families ends it.
  • No general capital gains tax. Residential land sold within 2 years is taxed under the bright line test; land dealing, financial arrangements and the FIF rules can tax specific gains.
  • Foreign Investment Fund (FIF) rules tax offshore shares on a deemed 5% return. A revenue account method for migrants who arrived after 1 April 2024 was enacted on 30 March 2026 (70% of realised gains plus dividends). A Bill introduced on 10 September 2026 would widen it and lift the de minimis from NZ$50,000 to NZ$100,000 from the 2026/27 year; not yet law.
  • India side: the India New Zealand double tax agreement (signed 17 October 1986, protocols 1997, 1999 and 2017) has a standard tie breaker: permanent home, centre of vital interests, habitual abode, nationality. An Indian citizen who is taxed in NZ escapes India's "deemed resident" rule for citizens earning over ₹15 lakh who are not taxed anywhere.

Sources: IRD tax residency, temporary tax exemption (17 Jun 2026), bright line and FIF pages; taxpolicy.ird.govt.nz Bill of 10 Sep 2026; incometaxindia.gov.in DTAA text.

9The India side: getting NZ$5M out lawfully

This is the hard part and the reason a Mumbai lawyer is worth more to this pathway than any NZ contact. While the investor is a "person resident in India" under FEMA, the Reserve Bank's Liberalised Remittance Scheme caps outward remittance at USD 250,000 per individual per Indian financial year (unchanged for 2025/26 and 2026/27), and the emigration rule expressly says forex above the limit may only be for incidental expenses and "not for earning points or credits to become eligible for immigration by way of overseas investments in government bonds; land; commercial enterprise; etc." Overseas direct and portfolio investment by an individual sits inside the same USD 250,000 cap; there is no separate quota.

The sequence Indian counsel will test

  1. While resident: LRS from every family member who will be a co owner

    Up to USD 250,000 each, paying 20% TCS above ₹10 lakh and recovering it in the return. Unused funds abroad must be repatriated within 180 days unless reinvested.

  2. Obtain NZ approval in principle, then leave India with the intention to settle

    Under FEMA section 2(v) a person who leaves India for an uncertain period with intent to stay outside becomes a "person resident outside India" from departure, regardless of day counts. Banks will test that intent against the NZ resident visa, home and family location.

  3. Redesignate accounts to NRO, liquidate Indian assets, pay Indian capital gains tax

    Resident accounts become NRO; demat holdings become non resident holdings; LRS is no longer available to a non resident.

  4. Repatriate USD 1M per person per financial year under the Remittance of Assets direction

    Straddle March and April to use two financial years within weeks. Spouse and adult children each have their own USD 1M if the assets are genuinely theirs.

  5. Keep the NZ paper trail identical to the nomination

    INZ requires the invested money to be the nominated funds or the proceeds of the nominated assets, arriving by SWIFT from the applicant's own accounts. A change of source mid course must be re nominated inside the 6 month window.

Timing tension to flag

INZ allows 6 months (12 with an extension) from approval in principle to land the money. The Indian route above needs the family to have left India and cleared two financial year ends for USD 2M per person. Counsel needs to sequence the NZ application date against the Indian financial year so the transfer window and the repatriation windows line up. This is precisely the work a Mumbai lawyer does and MAP does not.

11What MAP may and may not say

Under the Immigration Advisers Licensing Act 2007 anyone, anywhere in the world, who uses knowledge of immigration to advise, direct, assist or represent a person on a New Zealand immigration matter must hold an IAA licence unless exempt. The exemptions that matter are New Zealand lawyers with a current practising certificate (and their employees). An Indian lawyer is not exempt. The penalty is a fine up to NZ$100,000 and/or up to 7 years' imprisonment, whether or not a fee is charged.

Crew may

  • Repeat information that is publicly available or published by MBIE/INZ, exactly as published (this page is built for that).
  • Direct a person to INZ, Invest NZ, or a list of licensed advisers, and introduce them to our licensed adviser and NZ law firm partners.
  • Talk about MAP's own business: the export book, the tours, the producers a fund might invest in, hosting a visit to New Zealand.
  • Do clerical work, translation and settlement services, and record information the client provides onto a form if the "person assisting" declaration is signed.

Crew may not

  • Tell a person which category or visa they qualify for, or tailor any of this page to their circumstances. The IAA's own example of unlicensed advice is "using publicly available information to advise a person on an immigration matter".
  • Recommend a fund, promise an outcome, quote a timeframe as if it were ours, or describe any fund as "government approved" or "endorsed by Invest NZ" (FMC Act fair dealing).
  • Give Indian foreign exchange or tax advice. That is Tusshar's lane.
  • Charge for anything that looks like immigration advice, even bundled.
The one sentence crew script: "We are not immigration advisers. Everything we can tell you is on Immigration New Zealand's website, and we will introduce you to a licensed New Zealand adviser and a New Zealand law firm who will advise you. What we do is host you here, show you the businesses and the country, and, if you are a fund or a law firm, help you build the New Zealand relationships you need."

12How MAP assists, and how a lead moves through the website

MAP's role is introducer, host and relationship builder, never adviser. The commercial logic: a family that invests NZ$5M into a New Zealand fund that in turn backs the wineries, distilleries, honey producers and tourism operators in our CRM is the same family that buys our producers' products in India and takes our tours. The three lanes to build with Tusshar:

  1. Investor introductions

    Tusshar's Dubai clients who want a second passport are referred to MAP; MAP hosts the New Zealand visit (Travel builder), introduces the licensed adviser and NZ law firm, and introduces approved fund managers whose sectors match our producers. Referral fees, if any, must be agreed with the adviser and fund in writing and disclosed; never charged to the investor by MAP for advice.

  2. A New Zealand acceptable managed fund with an Indian and Gulf distribution channel

    An NZ resident manager (existing, or new with NZ control) applies to Invest NZ after 28 September 2026 under the new guidance; Tusshar's firm and Dubai fund act as distribution and back office partner under an investment management agreement; MAP's producer and tourism pipeline provides investee deal flow in food and beverage, agriculture, horticulture and tourism infrastructure, all named priority sectors. This is the "exclusive fund" analogue to what he has in Dubai.

  3. India side structuring

    Tusshar's firm advises on LRS, the emigrate first sequence, TCS recovery, NRO repatriation and Indian tax residency, which no New Zealand party can do. That is the reason he is essential and the reason to give him this material in full.
