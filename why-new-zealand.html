Why New Zealand

Pristine land.
Accessible premium.

New Zealand is the last significant landmass humans ever settled: two islands of rainforest, alps and coastline at the far edge of the world, shared by barely five million people. What grows here grows in air, water and soil most of the planet lost long ago. That purity is not a marketing line. It is the product.

Wine

France sells you its history. New Zealand sells you its land.

French wine is magnificent, and it costs like it. Its premium is centuries of reputation, complex classification, and vintages that swing with the weather. New Zealand's premium is simpler: the cleanest grape-growing environment on Earth, made by winemakers who answer to the vineyard rather than the appellation rulebook.

The old world

France

  • Premium defined by appellation and château hierarchy. The label costs more than the land
  • Significant vintage variation; great years priced beyond reach
  • Top Burgundy and Bordeaux now trade as luxury assets, not drinks
  • Centuries of intensive agriculture on ancient, worked soils
The new premium

New Zealand

  • 98% of vineyard area certified sustainable, the highest of any major wine nation
  • Maritime climate delivers consistency: the wine you loved is the wine you get
  • World-defining Sauvignon Blanc and world-class Pinot Noir at a fraction of equivalent Burgundy
  • Among the highest average bottle prices of any exporter, earned on quality, not heritage
Accessible premium, exclusive by nature: New Zealand makes barely 1% of the world's wine, and there is never enough of the best of it. A top single-vineyard bottle costs less than entry-level from a famous French appellation, drinks with more purity and honesty. And when it's gone, it's gone. Limited is the luxury.

Mānuka honey

Bees that feed on native bush found almost nowhere else on Earth

Mānuka is a native New Zealand tree. Our bees forage its blossom in remote hill country, much of it in Northland, far from industrial agriculture. The result is a honey with unique, scientifically measured properties that generic honey cannot imitate.

Everyone else's honey

Commodity, blended, often adulterated

Global honey trades as a commodity and is one of the world's most adulterated foods: cut with syrups, blended across borders, graded on little more than colour. Provenance is a label claim, rarely a test.

New Zealand mānuka

The only honey with a government science standard

New Zealand is the only country whose government defines authentic mānuka honey by laboratory test. Every export batch is verified against MPI's scientific definition, and grading systems like UMF™/MGO measure the unique compounds in every jar.

Under the NZ-India FTA, India cut mānuka tariffs 75% for New Zealand alone, the first concession of its kind India has given any country.

Craft beer & single malt

Alpine water, world-famous hops, and a whisky industry the world just noticed

Craft beer

Hops the whole world brews with

New Zealand hops (Nelson Sauvin, Riwaka, Motueka) are among the most sought-after in world brewing, grown in one small, sunny corner of the South Island. Our craft brewers use them fresh, with soft alpine water, in one of the world's most inventive small beer scenes.

Single malt whisky

World Whiskies Awards winners, already

New Zealand's young distilleries are beating the old world at its own game: Scapegrace has taken a world's-best single malt title, and Pōkeno and Cardrona are World Whiskies Awards category winners: whiskies built on glacial water and clean southern air.
